Two spongy organs with \~70 m² of gas-exchange surface, moved by the diaphragm — the only muscle whose rhythm is both automatic and fully voluntary, which is why breath is the universal doorway between traditions.

Two [[wp:Lung|lungs]] fill most of the chest and wrap the [[heart]]. Breathing is the one autonomic function you can take the wheel of, which is why so many recipes pass through this room.

## What people talk about here

Breath that will not drop past the collarbones is how [[anxiety]] describes itself. Breath counting, four in and six out, gets pinned here as a practice. [[long-covid|Long COVID]] and asthma threads describe air hunger. Chinese medicine files grief under the lung, and people who did not expect to find that useful often do.

## In the atlas

- Meridians: the [[lung-meridian]] starts near the shoulder and runs down the inside of the [[left-arm|arm]] to the thumb. Its partner is the [[large-intestine-meridian]]; the element is metal.
- Nerves: the [[vagus]] slows breathing; the [[sympathetic]] chain opens the airways.
- Centers: [[anahata]] sits between the lungs in the chakra map.
- Folk: Gemini rules the lungs and arms in [[astrology]]; the lung governs the [[skin]] and wei qi in [[chinese-medicine|Chinese medicine]].

Related rooms: [[heart]], [[throat]], [[skin]], [[autonomic]].

## Body

Bronchial tree, alveoli, pulmonary circulation. The diaphragm is driven by the phrenic nerve and its rhythm tunes blood pressure, heart rate (respiratory sinus arrhythmia) and vagal tone. The lungs also filter clots and metabolise hormones.

## Tradition

Chinese medicine: the Lung governs qi and breath, the skin and wei qi, and the emotion grief; paired with the Large Intestine (Metal). Ayurveda: seat of prana vayu and kapha. Mercury rules the lungs and Gemini the arms and chest.

## Evidence

Slow breathing measurably shifts autonomic balance; breath-based interventions have moderate evidence for anxiety, blood pressure and pain. Nasal breathing increases nitric oxide.

## Practice

Pranayama, box breathing, coherent breathing, Buteyko, Wim Hof; respiratory physiotherapy; singing.
