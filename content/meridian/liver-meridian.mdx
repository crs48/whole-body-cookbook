Yin channel of the Wood element, 14 classical points, most active 1–3 am in the organ clock.

The Liver [[wp:Meridian (Chinese medicine)|meridian]] is one of the twelve regular channels of [[chinese-medicine|Chinese medicine]]. Element: **wood**. Paired organ room: [[liver]]. Partner channel: [[gallbladder-meridian]].

## Route on the plate

From the big toe at LR-1 up the inside of the [[left-leg|leg]], through the groin and up the belly to the ribs below the [[liver]] at LR-14. LR-3 on top of the foot is the point people press for stuck qi. Drawn through Kidney-1, the knee, the trochanter, and the xiphoid, matching the spec's example polyline.

## What people pin

Liver qi stagnation as a name for [[depression]] that is a jam rather than a hole. Irritability. Tendons and [[eds|EDS]]. Menstrual threads.

## Tradition

Smooth flow of qi, blood storage, tendons and eyes. LR3 (Taichong) moves stagnation — irritability, PMS, tension headaches.

## Symbol

Emotion: anger, frustration. Element Wood; paired with its yang partner across the same element.

## Evidence

Meridians have no confirmed anatomical structure; many points coincide with nerve trunks, fascial planes and vessel bundles. Acupuncture has moderate evidence for chronic pain, nausea and some headache; point-specificity is disputed.

## Practice

Trace the channel with the hand in the direction of flow; acupressure on the listed points; stretch the channel (meridian yoga, Shin Tai).
