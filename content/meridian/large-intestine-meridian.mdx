Yang channel of the Metal element, 20 classical points, most active 5–7 am in the organ clock.

The Large intestine [[wp:Meridian (Chinese medicine)|meridian]] is one of the twelve regular channels of [[chinese-medicine|Chinese medicine]]. Element: **metal**. Paired organ room: [[intestines]]. Partner channel: [[lung-meridian]].

## Route on the plate

From the index finger at LI-1 up the outside of the [[right-arm|arm]] over the shoulder to the [[face]], ending beside the nose at LI-20. LI-4 in the web of the thumb is the most-cited acupoint in the cookbook.

## What people pin

Constipation and elimination threads that cross-link to the [[intestines|gut]]. Sinus and [[face]] notes. Headache pressure at LI-4.

## Tradition

Receives from the Small Intestine and eliminates; with the Lung it is about release. LI4 (Hegu) is the master point for the face and pain.

## Symbol

Emotion: grief held as holding on. Element Metal; paired with its yin partner across the same element.

## Evidence

Meridians have no confirmed anatomical structure; many points coincide with nerve trunks, fascial planes and vessel bundles. Acupuncture has moderate evidence for chronic pain, nausea and some headache; point-specificity is disputed.

## Practice

Trace the channel with the hand in the direction of flow; acupressure on the listed points; stretch the channel (meridian yoga, Shin Tai).
