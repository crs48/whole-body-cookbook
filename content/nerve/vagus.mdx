Cranial nerve X, the "wanderer": \~80 % sensory fibres carrying the body's state to the brain and the main parasympathetic output to heart and gut. Vagal tone is the physiological backbone of calm.

The [[wp:Vagus nerve|vagus]] is the tenth cranial nerve and the main line of the parasympathetic system. It leaves the brainstem, runs through the [[throat]], and wanders to the [[heart]], [[lungs]], [[stomach]], and [[intestines|gut]]. The atlas draws it as a dashed violet path through those landmarks.

## What people pin

Vagal tone, cold water, humming, long exhales, and gargling as folk regulation. [[wp:Polyvagal theory|Polyvagal]] vocabulary in [[cptsd|CPTSD]] and [[autism]] threads. Vasovagal fainting alongside [[pots|POTS]]. The gut-brain conversation in [[depression]]. The cookbook notes that polyvagal theory is contested as neuroscience and useful as a shared language.

## In the atlas

- Family: channels, group nerves. Off by default; the second layer to turn on after meridians.
- Its argument with the [[sympathetic]] chain is the [[autonomic]] field.
- Its lower reach overlaps the [[enteric]] system.

## Body

Nucleus of the solitary tract receives it; dorsal motor nucleus and nucleus ambiguus send it. It slows the heart, drives digestion, and signals inflammation to the brain (the inflammatory reflex).

## Tradition

Polyvagal theory splits it into ventral (social engagement) and dorsal (shutdown) branches — a clinically useful map, neuroscientifically contested. In yoga the vagus is increasingly identified with sushumna and the effects of pranayama.

## Evidence

Implanted VNS is approved for epilepsy and depression; taVNS through the ear and slow breathing measurably raise HRV. Vagal-tone claims for cold showers and humming are plausible but small.

## Practice

Extended exhale, humming, gargling, cold on the face, ear stimulation; the "vagus nerve" is the shared language of nervous-system regulation.
